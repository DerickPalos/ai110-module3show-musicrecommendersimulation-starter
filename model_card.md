# 🎧 Model Card: Music Recommender Simulation

## 1. Model Name  

**recomMEnd 1.0**

---

## 2. Intended Use  

recomMEnd is a mock music recommender meant to illustrate real-world recommender behavior at a smaller, local scale.

When a User has set their preferences, recomMEnd will generate recommendations from its bank of known Songs. Each Song will have a Score; the higher the Score, the more likely it is that the User will like the recommended Song. recomMEnd will also report the _reasons_ a Song was recommended.

---

## 3. How the Model Works  

Each Song has a few different attributes:

* Genre (e.g., Pop, Rock, Jazz, etc.)
* Mood (e.g., Happy, Sad, Relaxed, etc.)
* Energy - How lazy or energetic a song feels
* Tempo - How slow or fast a song is
* Valence - How sad or happy a song feels
* Danceability - How danceable a song is
* Acousticness - The percentage of acoustic instruments in the Song

Based on User preferences, recomMEnd will assign a score to each Song in its bank of known Songs. Genre and Energy are the most important and affect a Song's score the most; Valence and Mood are next, followed by Danceability and Acousticness.

The top `k` Songs with the highest score are closest to the User preference and are recommended.

---

## 4. Data  

The dataset `songs.csv` contains 50 songs.

Each Song has an ID, a Title, an Artist, and a Tempo (BPM).

The catalog spans 16 genres. There are a few large genres (pop, lofi, rock, jazz), a few medium sized genres (ambient, synthwave, indiep pop, hip hop, edm, folk), and a few niche/singleton genres (classical, metal, country, r&b, reggae, blues).

The catalog spans 10 moods. Each mood has multiple Songs associated with that mood.

Each Song also has Energy, Valence, Danceability, and Acousticness, all in the range [0.0-1.0].

## 6. Limitations and Bias 

Biggest weakness: genre and mood matches outweigh poorly matched features. The Conflicted profile (sad pop, with energy 0.95 and valence 0.2) shows it: the top result is Heartbreak Radio (4.88), a slow song with energy 0.45, because matching both genre and mood is worth a flat 2.5 points. Its 0.50 energy gap costs only 0.75 points, less than the mood bonus alone, so a song that is far from the requested feel can still win. Genre and mood are all-or-nothing, while every other feature gets partial credit, so the exact-match features carry about 42% of the maximum score. As a result, the system rewards a user's labels more than what they actually asked for, and the same pattern lets a single-song genre like classical win a "loud classical" profile despite an energy of 0.15.

---

## 7. Evaluation  

There were 5 User profiles generated by the AI assistant. Each profile gets a list of recommendations from each execution of `main.py`.

3 were typical User profiles: High-Energy Pop, Chill Lofi, and Deep Intense Rock. 2 were made specifically to test the scoring algorithm: Conflicted (wants sad pop, high energy) and Lonely Genre (wants classical, but loud). A (deleted) 6th profile was created and led to the implementation of case-insensitive matching.

With Conflicted, a slow song (energy 0.45) took the top spot even though the user asked for energy 0.95. Matching on genre and mood is worth 2.5 points, which was more than the hit from the energy mismatch.

With Lonely Genre, the only classical song in the dataset won even though it's very quiet, again because it matched on genre and mood.

When I adjusted the weights to make genre and mood worth fewer points, the top song stayed the same for all three normal users. Additionally, Conflicted no longer picked the slow song, and Lonely Genre picked a loud song.
